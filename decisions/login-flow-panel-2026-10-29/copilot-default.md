## Findings

- **[severity: blocker] The first company sign-in binds an unproven personal account to the company mapping, so the company's mapping can be bound to an XID the person doesn't own (Sign-in, "The first company sign-in" step 2).** The session credential just collects proofs, with no binding between the two legs. Attack on login CSRF: the attacker starts a personal Google flow and gets the victim to complete the company leg in the same browser or session, or the reverse. The victim's company subject then maps to the attacker's XID. The attacker's Scotty and personal side receive company-linked data, and the attacker can later mint people-tenant tokens as the "same person" as the victim. On a kiosk or shared browser, a leftover personal session gets silently adopted as "the personal account". Fix: both legs share one server-held nonce and session, the personal leg needs a *fresh* interactive auth (as linking does), and the user sees an explicit "bind this company account to this personal account" confirmation naming both accounts.

- **[severity: blocker] A new company member whose personal account already has an XID is mapped to that XID, so the company learns and controls a binding to a personal identity (step 2, Model).** The company gets a stable XID that also keys the person's personal side. Any other tenant that maps the same XID can be correlated. The XID acts as a global correlator across employers. Fix: tenants must not see the people-tenant XID. Use a per-tenant pairwise pseudonym, held in a kernel-side mapping from (tenant, pseudonym) to XID. That would also resolve Q5.

- **[severity: blocker] A malicious company admin can point tenant-held mappings at any XID, with nothing in the design that prevents it (Model, "Each company tenant holds its own mapping", Q5).** The company controls the mapping and it is tenant data. The admin writes (their IdP subject S) → victim XID. They sign in as S and get a company token whose XID is the victim's. Any kernel logic that derives access or identity from the XID can then be abused: Scotty acting on "this person", other tenants' membership lookups keyed by XID, and the MERGED redirect. They can also point a legit employee's mapping at a victim to learn their "membership" (oracle). Kernel must enforce: (a) a tenant can only bind a subject to an XID if the person *proved control of that XID in the same session* (a fresh personal proof whose link resolves to that XID), and never by an admin write; (b) a company token carries (tenant, pairwise id) and never confers people-tenant authority; (c) the mapping write API is kernel-mediated, with no raw admin edit of the XID column.

- **[severity: blocker] A company admin who controls the IdP can mint any subject, including recycled or new ones, and in the default auto-join mode becomes a member with an XID (Registered domains, first sign-in step 2).** Auto-create-by-default means the admin spawns unlimited identities. Linked with the previous point: if the "personal account" step accepts any proof, the admin can supply the personal leg using a Google account they control that was already linked to the victim XID? Not possible, since linking is refused on conflict. But the admin can pre-create their own personal accounts, so fresh XIDs are cheap. Keep company-minted XIDs distinct from personal ones: no cross-binding unless the person proves the personal side. And default to admin approval for tenants beyond a trivial size.

- **[severity: major] A recycled IdP subject or email can inherit a prior person's XID (Model, mapping, Q3).** For OIDC `sub`: Apple `sub` is stable per team but changes on developer-team transfer, and Google `sub` is stable. But generic OIDC and Entra IdPs may reassign `sub` or use email-like subjects. Entra's correct key is (tid, oid). A reassigned subject reaches the old person's company mapping, and if the old person had linked a personal account, the new person is mapped to that same XID and inherits the personal side and Scotty. Fix: keys are (exact issuer, tenant id, immutable subject). Refuse address-shaped subjects. On offboarding, flag the mapping `disabled` and do not allow its re-use for a new subject without admin plus person action. Never auto-attach.

- **[severity: major] The 30-day MERGED redirect is a takeover window and a stale-token window (Merges and stale mappings).** (a) A tenant that has not updated its mapping resolves to the live XID through the redirect, and the redirect decides authority. If a merge was malicious or mistaken, it is silently propagated to every tenant mapping. (b) After day 30 the redirect disappears. Unswept tenant mappings now point to a dead XID: the person is locked out of that tenant, or, worse, if "dead" is treated as "unknown", the next sign-in creates a *new* XID and duplicates membership. (c) XIDs are never reused, which is good, but the dead record should remain as a tombstone (xid → merged_into, hash only), as the previous panel recommended. Don't expire it; expire only the *auto-follow*. Make the redirect apply only to the person's own personal-link resolution, not to tenant-granted authority, and make the daily sweep failures alert.

- **[severity: major] Merges propagate through tenant mappings without the tenants consenting (Merges).** A merge event rewrites every tenant's mapping to the surviving XID. If merge is abused (the previous panel flagged "keep oldest" as attacker-selectable), a company with a mapping to the merged XID now maps its employee to the attacker's XID, or the attacker's identity now holds both memberships. Require fresh proofs of control of every mapping being merged, never an org-IdP proof alone, and tenants must NOT be allowed to fold in a merge that a company-side proof alone triggered.

- **[severity: major] The refuse rule depends on the email claim, and email is the wrong key for domain enforcement (Registered domains, Q2).** Cases:
  - *Google consumer accounts with an `@company` address:* `email_verified` is true for a gmail-alias-like or an unrelated Google account created with a company email (Google lets people create accounts on a domain they don't control and verifies by link). A person with a leftover non-Workspace Google account at `@company` is refused, which is intended, but a *stranger* who obtained the address after an employee left (recycled email) passes only if they can verify it. Treat `hd` rather than email domain as the Google signal.
  - *Entra B2B guests:* the guest's home tenant asserts the email, and `xms_edov` is not always present. Guests with `@company` addresses signed in through another tenant would be refused or admitted depending on the claim. Use `tid`, not the email, for Microsoft.
  - *Unverified emails:* "unverified has no domain" means an attacker's IdP that asserts an unverified `ceo@company` email bypasses the refuse rule and gets treated as a personal account whose displayed email says company. That's only an issue if anything downstream uses the email. Rule: never display or use an unverified email as identity.
  - *Email change at the IdP:* a person's email moves from `@personal` to `@company` after registration. The link is by subject so it persists, and the person escapes the refuse rule. Re-evaluate the domain on every sign-in, and suspend links on change.
  - *Subdomains:* `eng.company.com` or `company.com.evil` handling is unspecified. Define matching as exact registrable-domain with the public suffix list, and declare whether a verified `company.com` covers subdomains. Otherwise `user@sub.company.com` escapes.
  - *Enforcement by a different IdP:* the rule is only checked at the proof stage. A personal Apple "Hide My Email" relay address has no company domain and is never refused. That's inherent, so state it: the refuse rule is best-effort policy, not a security boundary. Security rests on tokens being per-tenant, not on the refuse rule.

- **[severity: major] Domain registration can be hijacked or go stale (Registered domains).** DNS-verified ownership "checked again periodically" means that when a domain lapses, is sold, or is hijacked via DNS, the new owner registers it and takes over the tenant lookup. Past members' "company sign-in" now points at the new owner's IdP. Add cooling-off and reserved names, and on domain ownership change drop the mapping and notify members. Also a registered company can claim a *competitor's* domain with a DNS slip. Also deny-list consumer issuers: `gmail.com`, `outlook.com`, `icloud.com`.

- **[severity: major] Suspending existing links on registration can lock a person out of their Scotty (Registered domains, Q4).** Someone whose only account is `@company` (Google Workspace they used personally before the company registered) loses the link the moment the company registers, unless the company authorized the same IdP. If the company authorizes a different IdP, that person's only identity is suspended and their Scotty is unreachable, while the company, by admin action, has effectively frozen a person's personal data. Also the admin can force it: register a domain, and every user of that domain is suspended. Fix: suspension must never remove the *person's* ability to recover. Notify before registration takes effect (grace period), and offer a "move my personal data to a new personal account" step, a one-time carve-out proven by the old link plus email control.

- **[severity: major] An org-owned account can become the permanent anchor of personal data (Linking and Scotty, first sign-in).** If a company-only person skips the personal step, a new XID is created and mapped, with Scotty's personal side hanging on a company IdP login that the company can disable. Offboarding then removes Scotty access despite "the personal side and Scotty are untouched", which is only true if a personal link exists. State that in the offboarding text and refuse people-tenant tokens from an org-classified IdP proof (classify with `hd`, `tid`, enterprise issuer).

- **[severity: major] "Linking" is fresh sign-in at the IdP but lacks login-CSRF protection and reauthentication details (Linking).** Attack: the attacker initiates a link flow for the attacker's own Google account in the victim's logged-in session (CSRF on the account chooser or a forced-login). The victim's XID gets the attacker's personal account linked. The attacker can then sign in as the victim: full takeover of Scotty and personal data. Fix: state parameter bound to the session, PKCE, `prompt=login` plus `max_age`, an explicit "link account X to your identity" confirmation showing the IdP account, and a notification to existing linked accounts with a delay or undo window for the new link.

- **[severity: major] Refuse-on-conflict is a denial-of-service and a squatting vector (Linking).** An attacker who links a victim's personal account to a throwaway XID first (possible if a Google account is compromised briefly, or via an XID created from a company proof for an address-shaped subject) makes the real owner's link fail forever, and "merge is a proven action" has no recovery described. Give the legitimate owner a path: proof of both XIDs, or a support recovery with a delay.

- **[severity: major] Multi-sign-in session credentials mix proofs, creating confused-deputy risks (first sign-in step 3).** "The session credential holds every proof made in the session": a proof made for a different purpose (company sign-in) could be replayed to mint a different tenant token, and a session left signed in on a shared browser carries all proofs. Fix: each proof carries audience/purpose, a nonce, and `auth_time`; a token minting call names exactly the tenant, and the kernel checks the proof is accepted by that tenant. Sign-out clears all proofs. Per-proof lifetimes, not a session-wide one.

- **[severity: minor] Offboarding "within minutes" is a window with no kick (Offboarding).** The previous panel asked for an admin immediate-kick and a max proof age. Add revocation of live company tokens by the kernel, not only by the IdP disabling. The IdP disable only blocks *new* mints.

- **[severity: minor] A lookup-by-typed-domain reveals which domains are registered, and error text distinguishes them (Sign-in, Custom).** "Your company isn't registered" is an oracle for customer enumeration. Rate-limit, return a uniform response for the lookup (redirect to the IdP either way if registered, a generic failure otherwise, within consistent timing), and don't reveal the tenant name before authentication.

- **[severity: minor] The remembered icon in local storage on a shared device records which company the previous user worked for.** Store only the domain, make it per-user opt-in ("remember on this device"), don't show it on a device that signed in through a "public computer" choice, and clear on sign-out with an obvious "forget".

- **[severity: minor] Tenant deletion deletes the mapping, but XIDs are kept about a year (Background).** A deleted tenant's mapping is gone, so a person who signs in again through a new tenant with the same IdP subject gets a new XID. Fine, but state it. Also a deleted tenant's domain gets released, which creates the re-registration risk above.

## Answers to the open questions

1. **Same-session personal proof is not enough on its own.** Bind the two legs with a single server-side nonce and session created at the start of the company sign-in. Require a fresh, interactive personal authentication (`prompt=login`, `max_age` of a few minutes), PKCE and state bound to the session, and a confirmation screen that names both accounts. Don't adopt a personal session that already exists in the browser. Offer a "this is a shared device" path that skips the personal link and creates a company-only XID.

2. **It narrows the hole but doesn't close it.** Use IdP-specific signals: Google `hd` plus `sub`; Microsoft `tid` and `oid`; never the email domain alone. Re-evaluate on every sign-in and on email change. Match registered domains exactly through the public suffix list, and decide explicitly whether subdomains are covered. Treat unverified email as no identity. Accept the refuse rule is a convenience and policy guard, and that per-tenant tokens carry the security.

3. **Remaining paths:** (a) a company admin who controls an IdP minting a recycled subject or email onto a mapping that already points at a personal-linked XID. Prevent by never auto-attaching a company proof to an existing XID without a fresh personal proof of that XID. (b) Login CSRF on linking. (c) A merge triggered by a proof from an org IdP alone. (d) The 30-day redirect: keep a permanent tombstone and make the redirect only a hint. (e) Recovery paths for locked-out owners, which attackers use as social-engineering targets: define them with delays and notifications.

4. **Yes, they can be locked out, and the design should not allow that.** Before registration takes effect, notify known users by email, give a grace period, and offer a one-time migration of the personal side to a personal account, proven by the existing `@company` link. Keep the suspended link usable *for personal-side access only*, and refused for company access, until the person has linked a personal account or the grace period ends, then prompt. Never suspend the only link on a person's XID without a recovery route.

5. **Yes, as written.** Kernel must enforce that a tenant cannot assign an XID: binding (tenant, subject) → XID requires a fresh proof of the XID in the same session, the tenant sees only a per-tenant pairwise identifier, and admin edits can only disable or delete a mapping, never retarget it. Also log all mapping writes. The kernel must intersect any requested tenant with live membership, as the earlier panel recommended.

6. **Reduce the correlation surface.** Pairwise per-tenant identifiers mean tenants never learn the XID or other memberships. The domain lookup should be rate-limited and respond uniformly, accepting that some enumeration of registered domains is unavoidable if sign-in works. The remembered icon should be opt-in, domain only, and cleared on sign-out. Tenants learn only the IdP subject, their pairwise id, and the email claims they already have.

## What is good and should stay

- Access is the token, not the XID; per-tenant tokens from proofs each tenant accepts.
- Personal data never falls back into a company tenant; Scotty refuses and asks.
- XIDs random and never reused; merges a separate proven action; refuse on conflicting links.
- OIDC only, no ad-hoc discovery, and no email entry on our side.
- DNS-verified domains with periodic recheck; suspend rather than delete on registration.
- Configuration in proto, and tenant mappings deleted with the tenant.

## Findings

- **[severity: blocker] “Same session” does not authorize binding two accounts.** On a kiosk, an attacker leaves their personal account active; the victim completes company sign-in, and their company subject becomes bound to the attacker’s XID. The reverse can attach an attacker’s company identity to the victim’s XID. Require fresh authentication of both selected accounts and explicit confirmation naming the pair. (§“The first company sign-in”, step 2.)

- **[severity: blocker] Fresh authentication of the new personal account does not authorize modifying the destination XID.** A malicious company admin maps their own company subject to a victim’s known XID, signs in, then links a previously unused attacker-controlled personal account. The flock conflict check passes; the attacker now has a personal sign-in to the victim’s Scotty. Require independent personal authority over an existing personal XID before adding a link; define a separate, protected initial-claim operation for company-only XIDs. (§“Model”; §“Linking and Scotty”.)

- **[severity: blocker] Accumulating every proof in one session can combine different humans’ authority.** Two tabs complete company sign-in for Alice and personal sign-in for Bob. If token exchange uses the session’s current XID with any retained acceptable proof, Bob’s personal proof could authorize Alice’s people-tenant token. Bind each proof to its authenticated principal, resolved XID, tenant, purpose and freshness; reject incompatible combinations. (§“The first company sign-in”, step 3.)

- **[severity: blocker] “Separate, proven action” leaves merge authorization undesigned.** An admin controlling a company identity must not use that identity’s mapping as proof of control over the mapped person’s personal XID. Otherwise, merging it into the admin’s XID launders company authority into personal access. Specify independent personal authorization for both personal identities, the disposition of every moved link, explicit consent, atomic execution and revocation of stale credentials. (§“Linking and Scotty”; §“Merges and stale mappings”; earlier panel synthesis, item 1.)

- **[severity: blocker] The multi-sign-in flow has no stated protection against callback substitution or session fixation.** An attacker can attempt to inject their personal-account callback into the victim’s pending company-binding operation, or exploit a fixed session that later collects victim proofs. Use separate, single-use transactions for each leg, bound to one explicit binding operation, with PKCE, state, nonce, expected issuer/client/tenant validation and session rotation. Parallel tabs must not overwrite pending operations. These protections follow the [OAuth Security BCP](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9700.html). (§“The sign-in page”; §“The first company sign-in”; §“Linking and Scotty”.)

- **[severity: major] Tenant-controlled mappings permit identity forgery inside that tenant unless kernel protects binding integrity.** An admin can point their subject at a coworker’s XID and potentially inherit XID-keyed records, roles or attribution. Company admins already control their IdP, so the platform cannot promise protection from company-local impersonation by that IdP. It can prohibit arbitrary XID assignments, require kernel-authorized bindings, and preserve the actual authenticating subject and issuer in audit records. (§“Model”; §“The first company sign-in”, step 1.)

- **[severity: major] The 30-day redirect assumes every stale reference will converge within a deadline.** A tenant offline for 31 days, a restored backup or a deliberately unmodified mapping loses resolution after redirect deletion. Failing closed causes lockout; treating the unknown XID as claimable creates a rebinding path. Retain minimal canonicalization records, including merge chains, for as long as references can survive. Never recreate an identity from an unresolved reference. (§“Merges and stale mappings”.)

- **[severity: major] Short-lived company tokens do not establish the promised offboarding deadline.** If kernel keeps exchanging an earlier company proof from the session credential, disabling the IdP account does not necessarily stop new platform tokens. A terminated employee can keep renewing access. Define proof expiration, membership revocation, configuration revisions and an immediate tenant-side disable operation; bound continued access from the last accepted authentication, not each platform refresh. (§“The first company sign-in”, step 3; §“Linking and Scotty”, “Offboarding”.)

- **[severity: major] “Verified email” is not a uniform proof of company affiliation.** Google documents that `email_verified=true` can persist for an external address whose mailbox ownership has changed; Workspace affiliation uses `hd`. Microsoft describes `xms_edov` as domain-owner verification, not an equivalent guarantee of an individual’s current employment. An obsolete consumer Google account can therefore trigger refusal without being a company account. Specify provider-specific semantics and keep domain routing separate from membership. [Google guidance](https://developers.google.com/identity/sign-in/android/backend-auth), [Microsoft claim reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity-platform/optional-claims-reference). (§“Registered domains”.)

- **[severity: major] Missing claims become a policy downgrade if “no domain” means “personal.”** A managed account authenticating through a provider that omits email verification could bypass company-account classification and become a personal anchor. Conversely, rejecting every account without verified email would exclude legitimate identities. Classify managed accounts from trustworthy provider context; unsupported or incomplete evidence must not silently acquire personal authority. (§“Registered domains”; §“Linking and Scotty”.)

- **[severity: major] Matching the authorized tenant does not establish eligibility for automatic membership.** An Entra B2B guest can authenticate in the authorized resource tenant and pass its `tid` check; default enrollment can then admit an unintended contractor or external guest. Define explicit guest, application-assignment and group eligibility rules. For Google, document how signed `hd` is bound to the configured organization/customer; issuer matching alone identifies Google generally. [Microsoft authorization guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity-platform/claims-validation), [Google OIDC documentation](https://developers.google.com/identity/openid-connect/openid-connect). (§“Registered domains”; §“The first company sign-in”, step 2.)

- **[severity: major] The refusal rule can prevent legitimate access to a second company.** Alice’s verified email is `alice@a.example`, but she signs into company B’s authorized Entra tenant as its invited guest. A literal domain-to-authorized-IdP rule refuses her because A owns the email domain. Distinguish enrollment as an A employee from explicitly authorized guest access to B; neither should establish a personal link. (§“Registered domains”.)

- **[severity: major] Domain registration can lock a person out of their only personal identity.** Someone used a company-address account for years before registration. Suspension removes their only people-tenant proof, while company sign-in grants only company access. “Suspended, not deleted” preserves data without providing recovery. Require advance migration and independently authorized recovery; company approval or access to a reassigned mailbox must not release Scotty. (§“Registered domains”; §“Linking and Scotty”.)

- **[severity: major] DNS ownership grants disruptive authority over historical users without a transfer policy.** A buyer of an expired domain can register it, select a new IdP and suspend old users’ personal links. DNS control today does not establish authority over yesterday’s people. Define ownership-transfer handling, notification and continuity rules; domain control must never authorize personal-link reassignment or recovery. (§“Registered domains”.)

- **[severity: major] Subdomain matching is unspecified and either interpretation has consequences.** Exact matching lets `user@division.company.example` escape a rule covering only `company.example`; suffix matching can place an independently delegated subsidiary under the parent tenant. Specify canonicalization, label-boundary matching, explicit subdomain scope and conflicting-registration precedence. A domain registration must not silently expand its authority. (§“The sign-in page”, “Custom”; §“Registered domains”.)

- **[severity: major] The design does not state an enforceable non-reassignment contract for subjects.** Reassigning an email with a new immutable subject must not reconnect the old XID. Reusing the same subject—or resetting the same IdP account for a successor—can inherit the old mapping. OIDC requires non-reassigned subjects, but a company-controlled issuer can violate that contract; it therefore cannot establish personal continuity. Pin identity namespaces and handle issuer/client migrations explicitly. [OIDC subject requirements](https://openid.net/specs/openid-connect-core-1_0.html#SubjectIDTypes). (§“Model”; §“Registered domains”; §“Linking and Scotty”.)

- **[severity: major] “Personal” is not defined strongly enough to protect Scotty from company administrators.** Apple, Google and Microsoft buttons explicitly accept business accounts. A managed account can become someone’s sole personal link before domain registration; an administrator who later resets that account gains personal access, or termination removes the person’s only recovery path. Provider branding and an unregistered email domain cannot establish independence from company control. (§“The sign-in page”; §“Registered domains”; §“Linking and Scotty”.)

- **[severity: major] Skipping personal sign-in predictably creates duplicate XIDs and forces risky merges.** Alice already has a personal XID but skips linking during company enrollment, creating another. Later, the flock correctly refuses her existing personal account because it belongs to the first XID. The ordinary onboarding path now requires the most dangerous identity operation. Treat company-only identities as provisional and provide an explicit reconciliation path; personal sign-in alone also does not restore company access after IdP loss. (§“The first company sign-in”, step 2; §“Linking and Scotty”.)

- **[severity: major] Binding and refusal invariants need transactional enforcement.** Two concurrent first sign-ins can observe no mapping and create different XIDs; concurrent personal links can both pass an application-level conflict check. A merge racing either operation can leave a link authorizing the wrong canonical identity. Require authoritative uniqueness, atomic conditional writes, idempotent completion and defined fail-closed behavior across bind/link/merge. (§“The first company sign-in”; §“Linking and Scotty”; §“Merges and stale mappings”.)

- **[severity: major] Global XIDs and merge watches expose a cross-company correlation mechanism.** Two companies can compare XIDs to correlate a worker. A malicious tenant that can insert arbitrary mappings could subscribe to a known person’s future merges and learn their successor XID. Expose tenant-specific identifiers, restrict watch eligibility to authorized bindings, and disclose no unrelated memberships, links or merge participants. Tenant deletion must also remove subscriptions and cached binding data. (§“Model”; §“Merges and stale mappings”.)

- **[severity: minor] Remembered icons and domain lookup reveal affiliations beyond an authenticated session.** A shared-device user sees the previous user’s company icon after logout; an automated client enumerates registered customers and their sign-in arrangements. Make remembering opt-in, offer shared-device clearing, minimize lookup responses and rate-limit enumeration. The lookup necessarily reveals some registration information; document that exposure. (§“The sign-in page”, “Custom”; §“Open questions for the panel”, question 6.)

## Answers to the open questions

1. **Require an explicit binding ceremony; session co-residence is insufficient.** Authenticate both selected accounts freshly, show their identities and the destination company, and commit through a short-lived, single-use server-side transaction. Protect each OIDC leg independently and bind both to that transaction. Existing personal XIDs require personal authorization; a company-derived XID reference provides none. Reject cross-tab substitution and conflicting principals.

2. **Keep the refusal intent, but replace the universal email test with provider-specific policy.** Separate domain routing, managed-account classification and tenant membership. Validate the exact configured issuer, audience and organization context; explicitly authorize guests and enrollment eligibility. Define missing-claim behavior, domain changes and subdomain scope. Allow authorized guest access to another company without converting that proof into a personal anchor.

3. **Make personal identity mutations depend on independent personal authority.** Adding a link requires authorization over the destination as well as fresh control of the new account. Merges require independent authorization for both personal identities, an explicit link-disposition policy, atomic updates and stale-credential handling. Reject email-based continuity and unsupported subject reassignment. Keep durable merge resolution records; a 30-day deadline cannot establish safety.

4. **Provide migration before suspension and a recovery path independent of company administrators.** Notify affected users while their existing personal authentication still works and let them establish an independent anchor. Preserve personal data if migration is incomplete. After suspension, company sign-in, DNS ownership and mailbox control must not unlock the historical personal identity; users without sufficient independent evidence need a defined recovery process.

5. **Let companies control membership and roles, while kernel controls identity bindings.** Tenant-held rows must not be authoritative arbitrary assignments to global XIDs. Kernel should accept only authorized bindings, retain their provenance and revision, and prevent them from granting personal or other-tenant authority. A malicious IdP administrator can impersonate users within its own trust domain; audit records must distinguish the actual company proof from any claimed global identity.

6. **Keep global identity internal and disclose only what each tenant needs.** Use tenant-specific external identifiers, expose no membership graph, and restrict merge notifications to legitimate existing bindings. Make device remembering optional and easy to clear. Minimize and rate-limit domain lookup while acknowledging that registration discovery remains observable. Do not expose personal account addresses or provider links to company admins merely because a binding exists.

## What is good and should stay

- Tenant-scoped tokens derived from proofs that the destination tenant accepts.
- XIDs that are random, never reused and insufficient by themselves for access.
- Personal and company links kept in separate authority domains.
- Conflicting personal links refused rather than silently merged.
- Scotty requiring a people-tenant token, with no company-storage fallback.
- DNS-verified registration, configured OIDC providers and repeated lookup of remembered domains.

## Findings

- **[blocker]** An org login that hits an already-mapped key signs in as the XID, so the org IdP admin, or a recycled org `sub`, becomes the person and reaches their personal data. Huddle #3 and #4 give org keys a path to the XID but never say an org-only login must be weaker. Scotty's data under `/users/<xid>` is exactly what #3 says it protects. Login-model §3 kept per-tenant tokens so that "each tenant sees only calls made with its own token."
- **[blocker]** The merge in #13 has no authorization rule, atomicity, or reference handling. It doesn't say which proofs are required (both XIDs' owners, freshly?). It also doesn't say whether an org-IdP proof can trigger it. If one can, an org admin can absorb an employee's XID. Keep-oldest is not trust-ordered: a device-anchored XID can outrank a Google-anchored one, and an attacker holding one compromised mapping can fold in a victim's XID. Deleting the loser (#13 plus #14) dangles grants, `/users/<xid>` data, SPIFFE paths, and live tokens whose `sub` is now dead.
- **[blocker]** The single `tenants`-claim token (#8) removes audience scoping and replaces the isolation in tenant-decisions #9c and login-model §3. Every tenant service receives a bearer token that names all the user's other tenants, which leaks competitor and customer relationships. It is also replayable against any other tenant's service, so a compromised tenant service can act in the others. Per-audience tokens (RFC 8693 exchange) fix both while keeping "one XID".
- **[blocker]** Self-service tenant creation (#10) hands admin to whoever logs in first. That can be an Entra guest, a contractor, an intern, or a former domain owner. Google `hd` follows domain ownership, not the company, so a lapsed domain gives a new owner the tenant. Microsoft `tid` also covers B2B guests. D29 B2 deliberately removed claim-to-tenant routing, and #10 restores it with no membership policy and no authorized list (login-model §6.4, D29 §3.3). The side letter admits the first-admin role is unassigned, but it is the central security decision.
- **[major]** Item #12's claim that an attested key works "across devices through the shared keychain" is contradictory. App Attest and Android hardware-attested keys are non-exportable and per-device or per-install. Login-model §2a's synced keys are self-asserted and cannot be attested. Attestation proves a genuine app on genuine hardware, not a human. Every reinstall or restore yields a new key, so a new XID, and Sybil XIDs are cheap. The issuer must also be keyed by (root, key id) with bundle-ID pinning.
- **[major]** Personal anchors don't separate org-owned accounts from consumer ones. A Workspace account (`hd` present), an Entra account, or a Managed Apple ID could become the permanent personal mapping in #3. That recreates the "termination takes Scotty away" problem #3 exists to avoid. #3 and #4 list Google, Apple, and Microsoft in both roles with no classifier. Apple also has no `hd` or `tid` analog, so "Apple org accounts" (#4) cannot bind to a TID.
- **[major]** The immutable-claim table in #2 is incomplete or wrong. Microsoft: #2 says `oid`, but D29 §3.1 keys on `sub`, which is pairwise per app. `oid` is stable across apps, but it is per-tenant for guests, so the key should be (`tid`, `oid`). Google: only `sub` is immutable, and `email` and `hd` are not. Apple: `sub` changes on a team transfer, and email is only sent on first login. Generic OIDC: some IdPs use email as `sub`, and issuer URLs change. Tenant binding keys (`hd`, `tid`, issuer) and person keys are different types and need separate treatment.
- **[major]** The soft-state membership in #4 has a hole and a cost. If the platform's own refresh renews the session, an offboarded employee never ages out, so lifetime must hang on the IdP-verified `auth_time`. The lifetime is unspecified (Open list), so it is unbounded. There is no admin kick or immediate revoke, and active employees who log in rarely lose membership. Dropping D29's `max_proof_age`, `mapped_roles`, and the revocation fields also drops roles, which "TIDs only" leaves nowhere to hold.
- **[major]** Revocation latency equals token lifetime across clusters (#8), yet #9 lets the kernel authorize a write from the claim alone. The kernel holds live membership, so there is no reason to trust a stale claim. There is no `jti` or epoch denylist, so a compromised account stays usable until expiry.
- **[major]** The kernel/Stratum split (#11) leaves failure modes undefined. A stale positive in the kernel cache mints tokens for a merged or deleted XID or a revoked mapping. A miss treated as absence mints a duplicate XID. No behavior is stated for a Stratum outage or partition. Stratum is not yet in the quorum equation (side letter), so the central invariant has no designed backing.
- **[major]** Dropping tombstones (#14) conflicts with merge, with TIDs, and with restore. A backup restore can resurrect deleted XIDs and revoked mappings. #6 says TIDs are never reused but gives no mechanism. When a tenant name is released and re-claimed, the new owner can inherit trust that was bound to the name.
- **[major]** Tenants with no org IdP (side letter) have no creation path, since #10 only starts from an org login. Login-model §5 already had one: a customer's device principal is promoted to admin of a new tenant. The huddle silently drops customers and tenant-to-tenant links, which tenant-decisions and login-model §6.6 rely on.
- **[major]** Privacy, audit, and abuse are missing. XID, `sub`, and issuer mappings are personal data under GDPR/CCPA, and "permanent" personal mappings (#3) have no erasure path. It is unspecified who may delete an XID, or what happens to tenant-owned records the XID authored or to a tenant whose last admin is deleted. There is no append-only log of bind, unbind, merge, or tenant-creation events. There are no limits on XID minting, tenant creation, or name claims, and no unicode normalization or confusable handling on names.
- **[minor]** Item #9 has the client name the namespace, while tenant-decisions #2 says tenant comes "ONLY from the SPIFFE ID, never a request field". This is safe only if the kernel intersects the claimed TID with the live membership and the service's own tenant.
- **[minor]** Item #5 lumps self-asserted synced keys with attested keys under the same mapping type. They differ in assurance, so the `acr` level must be visible to policy.
- **[minor]** Item #3's bounce flow is open to login CSRF. An attacker can bind their personal sign-in to a victim's pending org login unless both are tied to one nonce and session.

## Recommendations

1. Add an assurance level to every token, such as `amr` or `acr`: personal-proven, device-proven, or org-only. An org-only login must not reach `/users/<xid>`, or must get a tenant-scoped token only.
2. Make merge a two-sided proof. It needs fresh personal or device proof for both XIDs, and never an org-IdP proof. Execute it as one Stratum transaction that leaves a redirect record (`old → new`) and an audit entry. Choose the survivor by assurance, then age.
3. Reduce how often merge is needed: mint device-first and add personal IdPs as upgrades of the same XID (login-model §2b), as D29 does for people.
4. Replace the single token with a session token (`sub` = XID, no `tenants`) and per-TID tokens minted by exchange. Each carries `aud`, `tid`, and role, and none names other tenants.
5. Keep a live membership record in Stratum with `proof_issuer`, `proved_at`, `mapped_roles`, expiry, and a revoked flag. Bind soft-state lifetime to org `auth_time`, give admins an immediate kick, and have the kernel check live membership on writes.
6. Gate self-service creation. Require DNS or admin-consent verification of the domain (Google `hd`, Entra `tid` with an admin or `wids` signal). Create the tenant "pending" and make the first admin explicit. Reject guests, and decide what shared-domain issuers do.
7. Classify accounts at login (Google `hd`, Entra `tid` versus the consumer tenant, Managed Apple ID). Never accept an org-owned account as a personal anchor.
8. Fix the claim table. Microsoft key = (`tid`, `oid`). Google key = `sub`, with `hd` verified and treated as mutable. Apple key = `sub` plus team ID, handling transfer. Generic OIDC = the tenant picks the subject claim, and email is refused. Add issuer aliasing.
9. State the attestation story honestly. Attested keys are per-device, so syncing them is a separate self-asserted key. Pin bundle ID, rate-limit minting per device and per root, and rely on attestation for anti-abuse only. It cannot prove "human" or replace recovery.
10. Specify Stratum: linearizable compare-and-swap, multi-key transactions, per-record revision, change watches, and a restore that replays tombstones. Define the kernel as fail-closed for first-seen keys, with a bounded stale-serve window and watch-driven invalidation. Resolve the quorum question before building.
11. Keep minimal tombstones, holding only the XID or TID hash plus the deletion time, to block resurrection and to prove erasure. Add a name-release cooling period.
12. Write the product rules: erasure and export flows, last-admin protection and admin transfer, an audit event schema, per-IP and per-key rate limits, and name normalization and reserved lists.
13. Restore the customer-promotion path for IdP-less businesses, and keep tenant-to-tenant links in the graph.
14. Reconcile #9 with tenant-decisions #2. Intersect the requested TID with live membership and the service's own tenant.

## What is sound

- XIDs are opaque, minted by us, and never derived from email (#1, #14). Keying mappings on (issuer, immutable subject), never email, is correct, particularly given Apple's relay addresses.
- Tenants are keyed by an immutable TID, with names as separate many-to-one mappings (#6), so renames and reuse don't break grants.
- Services stay fixed and chroot-by-identity is unchanged (#7). Tenant-decisions #2 and #3 remain the right model.
- Routing uniqueness checks to Stratum first (#11) is the right call, because only a durable atomic store can enforce them.
- The personal-first anchor (#3), with its Scotty rationale, is a good principle. It needs the account classifier in recommendation 7.
- Making merge an explicit operation (#13) is better than "never merge, refuse", if it gets the proofs in recommendation 2.
- Dropping the D29 tenant-door-as-second-identity model and treating org logins as membership proof (#4) is consistent with D29 I3.

## Findings

- **[blocker]** The merge operation (huddle item 13) has no authorization model, which makes it the cheapest account-takeover path in the design. Nothing in item 13 says who may request a merge or what proof is required; if proving control of *one* of the two XIDs suffices, an attacker who controls any single mapping — a device key, a throwaway Google account, an org account at a shared-domain issuer — can fuse their XID into a victim's and inherit every org mapping and `/users/<xid>` namespace. Merge must require live, recent proof of control of **every** mapping being moved, plus re-auth on the surviving XID.
- **[blocker]** "Keep the oldest XID" is an attacker-selectable rule. Combined with self-service minting from attested devices (item 12), an adversary can pre-mint XIDs cheaply and hold them, so the *attacker's* XID is the survivor and the victim's record is the one deleted. Survivorship should be chosen by the authenticated human at merge time, defaulting to the XID that currently holds org memberships, never by timestamp.
- **[blocker]** Item 12's "attested device, shared across the user's devices through the shared keychain" is not implementable. App Attest and Google hardware attestation keys are hardware-bound and non-exportable; a synced-keychain key is by construction *not* attested — which item 5 itself gets right ("ours for self-asserted keys (synced keychain)") before item 12 collapses the two. Pick one per mapping and carry an explicit assurance level; an attested mapping is per-device, a synced mapping is cross-device and self-asserted.
- **[blocker]** One token carrying a `tenants` claim (item 8) reintroduces the cross-tenant blast radius that the 2026-09-08 record and `login-model-2026-09-08.md` §3 deliberately removed. That record's class 9c holds "one token per tenant membership" and §3 states the Tool Box split "is enforced by the kernel, not by the router being careful" — item 8 makes it exactly the router being careful, since any tenant service or compromised downstream that captures the token can act in sibling tenants. Keep one user token as the subject credential, but make every outbound call use an audience-scoped per-tenant token (the existing `service_auds`/`service_tokens` path).
- **[blocker]** Soft-state org membership (item 4) leaves no revocation path at all, only expiry. D29 §3.2.3 carries `proved_at_ms` + `max_proof_age` *and* explicit revocation on `TenantMembership` (R3: "an admin revocation in our system is immediate"); item 4 deletes the record, so an offboarded employee — or a terminated admin — retains tenant access for the full lifetime with no lever. Enterprise tenants will not accept "ages out eventually."
- **[blocker]** Self-service tenant creation bound to an issuer (item 10) hands whole consumer populations to one founder. Microsoft's consumer MSA tenant id, Google accounts with no `hd`, `outlook.com`, and every university or hosting-provider domain are single issuer/`hd` values; the side letter notes universities but not that the first person to sign in with a personal Microsoft account would found the tenant containing everyone else's. Require a deny-list of consumer/shared issuers plus proof of domain control (DNS TXT) before an issuer→TID binding confers membership or admin.
- **[blocker]** Item 11 specifies Stratum as "uniqueness check plus write atomic" — a single-key CAS — but merge is a multi-key transaction (N mapping rebinds, one XID delete, membership moves). A partial merge leaves mappings pointing at a deleted XID, i.e. permanent lockout with no tombstone to diagnose it. Stratum must offer a linearizable multi-key transaction, and the design must state read-your-writes for the kernel at mint, max tolerated staleness, and fail-closed behaviour when Stratum is unreachable — otherwise every login depends on an undesigned component (its own side letter admits it is "not yet in the quorum equation").
- **[major]** Removing tombstones (item 14) breaks referential integrity, not just reuse. Random XIDs make collision a non-issue, but tokens in flight with `sub` = a deleted XID, grants held by third parties, SPIFFE paths, audit rows and Stratum/kernel copies all need "this XID merged into that one" or "this XID is gone"; with no tombstone the kernel cannot distinguish a merged identity from a forged one. Keep a minimal pseudonymous tombstone (`xid → {merged_into | deleted_at}`) even after personal data is shredded.
- **[major]** Microsoft's immutable key is wrong as written (item 2). `oid` is unique within a directory, not globally; the key must be `(iss including {tid}, oid)`, and B2B guests legitimately produce a *different* `oid` per tenant, so one human predictably yields several XIDs — which is the merge pressure item 13 was invented to relieve. This also silently reverses D29 OQ4, which took verbatim `sub`; say which and why.
- **[major]** "Generic OIDC `sub`" is not a safe immutability assumption. Keycloak and Okta emit stable UUIDs, but ADFS-era and misconfigured issuers emit email or UPN as `sub`, which rebinds a departed employee's identity to their replacement. Per-tenant IdP registration should require an explicit attestation that `sub` is immutable and non-reassigned, and reject issuers whose `sub` looks like an address.
- **[major]** Attestation does not prove humanity (item 12's first stated reason). App Attest proves an unmodified app on genuine Apple hardware; device farms and corporate MDM fleets produce unlimited genuine attestations, so it is weak Sybil resistance at best and no proof of a distinct person. If the goal is abuse control, say rate-limiting and cost, not personhood.
- **[major]** The no-IdP business (side letter 2) is the modal SMB customer, and invite-based membership cannot be "soft state refreshed by an org login" — there is no org login. That forces a second membership kind, contradicting item 4's "there is no separate membership record," and the invite path needs expiry, binding to a specific XID at accept, and admin re-auth. Item 4's elegance is bought by excluding most of the market.
- **[major]** Roles, admin assignment, and per-tenant door policy disappear relative to D29 §3.3. D29 had `required_door`, `max_proof_age_seconds`, `auto_join`, and exact-match `claim_mappings`; the huddle has no place to record "this tenant requires an Okta proof no older than 7 days," because the mapping *is* the membership. Re-admit a thin per-tenant membership policy slice.
- **[minor]** Tenant name rebinding (item 6) needs quarantine. "Each name bound to one TID at a time" lets a released name be re-claimed, so cross-tenant links and human recognition transfer to a new owner; add a reserved list and a cooling-off period.
- **[minor]** The `tenants` claim is unbounded. The viral customer path (`login-model` §5) expects people who are customers of many tenants; a claim that grows per membership eventually breaks header limits. Bound it, or carry a reference plus the tenant being addressed.
- **[minor]** No audit, no rate limiting, no recovery anywhere in the huddle. Merge, issuer binding, name claim and mapping unbind are the privileged operations and none is specified as audited; `login-model` §7 left recovery open, and the huddle now puts *business* access behind the same unrecoverable anchors.

## Recommendations

1. Make merge an explicit, audited, multi-key Stratum transaction: require fresh re-auth on every mapping being moved, let the user choose the survivor, write a `merged_into` tombstone, and bump an identity epoch that invalidates outstanding tokens.
2. Split token roles: `sub`=XID token authenticates the human; all tenant-directed calls use short-lived audience-scoped tenant tokens derived from it. Keep the `tenants` claim only as a routing hint, never as authority.
3. Restore an explicit membership record with `revoked_at`, `proved_at_ms`, and a per-tenant `max_proof_age` — keep item 4's "org login is the proof," but give admins an immediate kill switch and support optional SCIM/CAEP for tenants that want it.
4. Gate issuer→TID binding behind a consumer-issuer deny-list (Microsoft consumer tid, no-`hd` Google, major mail domains) plus DNS domain-control verification; self-service creates an *unverified* tenant with no cross-user membership until verification passes.
5. Define mapping assurance levels explicitly: `attested_device` (hardware-bound, per-device), `synced_key` (cross-device, self-asserted), `personal_idp`, `org_idp` — and allow XID minting only from levels you name, with device-only XIDs flagged as unrecoverable in-product.
6. Correct the key table: Microsoft `(iss{tid}, oid)`, Apple `sub` with a documented team-transfer hazard, generic OIDC gated on a declared-immutable `sub`.
7. Write the Stratum contract before anything else: linearizable multi-key transactions, unique secondary indexes on mapping keys, read-your-writes at mint, defined fail-closed login behaviour, and inclusion in the quorum equation.
8. Add the operational chapter the huddle omits: audit log for every graph mutation, rate limits on XID/TID creation and merge, a deletion design that crypto-shreds personal data while retaining pseudonymous audit, and tenant name quarantine.
9. Specify the invite-based membership kind now rather than deferring it; the plumber-on-gmail case is the common one and it determines whether item 4 survives.

## What is sound

- XID living outside tenants, keyed by `(issuer, immutable subject)` and never email — this correctly supersedes D29's Person at `/tenants/people` and respects the Apple relay-address lesson from `login-model` §2.
- One mapping key → at most one XID, with a conflicting login refused rather than silently joined; the merge fix belongs around this invariant, not instead of it.
- TID indirection with names as separate, rebindable mappings, and paths/grants/claims using the TID.
- Stratum-first for every invariant-bearing write, with the kernel copy as a convergent cache — the right division, needing only a stronger contract.
- The personal-first rule and its stated reason (the worker keeps Scotty through termination); it is the one place the design puts a human interest ahead of mechanism.
- Services stay fixed and the 2026-09-08 chroot rule is untouched — only users move. Do not reopen this.
- Item 9's "writes name their namespace and the kernel checks the claim," which preserves D29's I1 (OPA never names a tenant for a human).
